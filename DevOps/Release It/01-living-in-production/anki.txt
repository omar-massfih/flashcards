Why is "feature complete" not the same as "production ready"?	Feature completion proves the system does something; production readiness proves it can keep doing it under real load, failure, and operational constraints.
Why is passing QA a poor proxy for production readiness?	QA shows that known scenarios work; production tests whether the system survives unknown traffic patterns, hostile inputs, and long-running operational stress.
Why should software design focus on what a system must not do, not only what it should do?	Users and businesses suffer more from crashes, data loss, security failures, and outages than from missing a small feature, so failure behavior is part of the design.
Why is "design for production" analogous to "design for manufacturability"?	Both treat downstream operation as a first-class design constraint so the product can be delivered and run at low cost and high quality.
Why can optimizing for development speed alone be financially irrational?	A one-time shortcut in development often creates recurring operational costs, and long-lived systems spend far more time being operated than being built.
Why can investments in safer deployment mechanisms have outsized ROI?	They replace repeated downtime costs with a one-time engineering cost, so even modest reliability improvements compound over years of releases.
Why do early architectural decisions have outsized long-term effects?	Early boundaries and decompositions harden into team structure, funding, and delivery processes, making them expensive to reverse once the organization forms around them.
Why does getting software into production early help architecture?	Production is where real constraints finally appear, so early release turns unknowns into feedback before bad assumptions become entrenched.
Why does pragmatic architecture focus on change instead of an ideal end state?	Systems live under shifting load, business needs, and failure modes, so architecture should make improvement, deployment, and measurement easier over time.
Why is software's real value delivered only in production?	Everything before production is preparation; the system helps users and creates business value only when it runs successfully in the real world.
