Why is DNS described as a distributed, hierarchical, and eventually consistent key-value store?	It maps names to values across many cooperating servers, resolves names by climbing a hierarchy, and relies on caches that may temporarily disagree with the latest state.
Why does DNS resolution use iterative lookup through a resolver instead of having browsers query every name server directly?	The resolver centralizes the multi-step discovery process, caching, and protocol handling so clients do not need to know the whole hierarchy themselves.
Why is the address of a root name server enough to start resolving any hostname?	The DNS hierarchy lets each level delegate the next one, so knowing where the roots are allows a resolver to discover TLD servers, then authoritative servers, and finally the target record.
Why is caching so important to DNS performance and scalability?	Without caches, every lookup would trigger multiple network round trips and much higher global load on upstream name servers.
Why does DNS use TTLs on records?	TTLs bound how long caches may treat an answer as valid, balancing freshness against lookup cost and name-server load.
Why is choosing a DNS TTL a trade-off?	Long TTLs reduce lookup load but slow propagation of changes, while short TTLs speed change visibility but increase latency, cache misses, and pressure on name servers.
Why can DNS remain eventually inconsistent even after a TTL has expired?	Some clients ignore or mishandle TTLs, so stale answers can persist in the wild longer than the authoritative intent and operators cannot assume TTL expiry means every client has refreshed.
Why can DNS become a single point of failure for an application?	If clients cannot resolve the application's name, they cannot even attempt to connect, no matter how healthy the application servers are.
Why can serving stale DNS data be more robust than serving no data at all during an outage?	Name-to-address mappings usually change slowly, so a slightly old answer often keeps the system usable, whereas refusing to answer breaks discovery completely.
